Section 1 · The Book

Please “Don’t” Help

An obstacle-race comedy about a man on forearm crutches who would rather carry the sandbag alone, and the paper friends who refuse to let him.

A satirical e-book, currently being drafted. Muscle Hero trains for Spartan-style obstacle races and works hard at never asking anyone for anything. Chapters cover advanced techniques such as Pretending You Meant To Do It That Way, The Fourteen-Trip Grocery Haul, and How To Reply "I'm good!" While Hanging From the Monkey Bars.

Section 2-A · Persons of Interest

The cast, cut from paper

In narrative therapy, the person is not the problem. The problem is the problem. So the author cut his fears, habits and hang-ups out of paper, gave them names, and let them argue with him in public. It is much easier to laugh at a fear when it is wearing a cardigan.

MH

Muscle Hero

The author, on forearm crutches, in a cape. Puts up walls when he's scared to show his heart. Undefeated at planking, in his head.

FF

The therapist. Narrator. Asks the question you were hoping nobody would ask.

EB

Eric the Bull

Best friend, male nurse, punching bag. The one whose approval Muscle Hero keeps waiting for. Shows respect with one hoof tap.

Section 2-B · Course Map

Every obstacle needs a friend

Muscle Hero can plank, crawl and grip with the best of them. The hard part comes at each obstacle, where someone offers to help. The book follows him from the backyard training course to race day, one obstacle and one accepted hand at a time.

Mud Pit
Eric the Bull hauls him out by the cape and says nothing. One hoof tap. Muscle Hero thinks about it for three chapters.
Monkey Bars
Marigold counts every rung out loud like it is the most amazing thing she has ever seen. He makes it two rungs further than usual.
Plank Station
The Mice hold up scorecards. All tens. He asks for a recount.
Sandbag Carry
Foxy Fax asks one question: "Who decided you carry this alone?" He has no answer, and sets the bag down to think.
Crutch Lane
Dot the Sloth walks beside him at exactly his pace. Neither of them is in a hurry. That is the whole point.
Cargo Net
Trendy and Bendy Wendy blows the whistle and yells "TEAMWORK IS NOT A PENALTY!" She is technically correct.

Section 5 · Sample Excerpt · Draft

From Chapter 3: "I'm Good!"

The phrase "I'm good!" is the Swiss Army knife of the self-reliant. It works when you are good. It works better when you are not. Spoken with the right brightness, it can end a conversation, deflect a casserole, and turn away a neighbor holding the exact ladder you need.

Muscle Hero says it eleven times a day. Each time, a small gray mouse in the corner holds up a card: TRANSLATION: NOT GOOD. Foxy Fax writes this down. Dot the Sloth begins a thumbs-up that will finish sometime Thursday. Eric taps one hoof, and Muscle Hero spends the rest of the afternoon wondering what it meant.

Advanced practitioners add their own thumbs-up. Masters do it while holding a dresser on one knee. If anyone offers to take the other end, refer to Chapter 7: Saying "I've got it" Through Gritted Teeth.
